Climatological Lapse Rates and Freezing-Level 
Dynamics in Caucasus Mountain Network
Grace Sohn
Mentors: Rob Hellstrom (Geography) & Misha Elashvili (Mathematics)
Bridgewater State University
Abstract
This research examines long-term temperature-elevation relationships 
and freezing-level variability using weather station data across an 
elevation gradient from the Caucasus and Andes mountain networks. 
Daily observations (raw datasets) were quality-controlled and 
aggregated into seasonal and annual climatologies. Linear regression 
between temperature and elevation was used to estimate lapse rates 
and freezing-level altitude. Results indicate stable but seasonally 
distinct lapse-rate behavior, with stronger gradients during the cool 
season and greater variability over time. Freezing-level altitude shows 
an overall increase over the years with significant interannual variability, 
highlighting potential implications for glacier melt and 
thermodynamically warm years. The analysis establishes a baseline 
framework for evaluating elevation-dependent climate change using 
observational station networks.
Methods
Data Cleaning & Processing
Daily weather station observations were quality-controlled and 
standardized prior to analysis. Sentinel missing values (−9999.0) 
were converted to NaN, and rows containing no valid 
meteorological information were removed. Variables, units, and 
timestamps were harmonized across stations.
Data Aggregation
Cleaned daily data were aggregated into monthly and annual 
summaries. 
Data Completeness Audit
A completeness assessment was performed to identify 
analyzable time periods. Months with insufficient observations 
were flagged as missing, and annual values were calculated 
only when adequate monthly coverage existed. Analyses were 
restricted to years with sufficient overlapping data across 
stations.
Lapse Rate Modeling
Regression: Fit z = m × T + b across stations
o T = that year’s annual mean at each station
o z = station elevation
o b = freezing level ( intercept at T = 0 °C) 
Lapse rate (°C km−1^{-1}−1) was derived as: LR=−1000/m
Freezing-Level Estimation
Freezing-level altitude was defined as the elevation 
corresponding to T=0°C, equal to the regression intercept 
(FL=b). Annual time series of lapse rate and freezing level were 
analyzed to evaluate variability and long-term trends.

- Long-term Steepening of Annual LR: The weak positive 
trend of annual LR suggests a gradual strengthening of 
temperature with elevation  consistent with high elevations 
warming faster (Elevation Dependent Warming).
- Strong Seasonal LR Contrast: The cool season LR is 
consistently greater than the warm season LR across nearly all 
years  indicates stronger gradients in cool season due to 
inversions and cold-air pooling. This likely reflects enhanced 
wintertime atmospheric stability at low elevations, which is
consistent with broader Northern Hemisphere circulation 
shifts and ENSO-related variability during this period. In 
contrast, warm season LR remain relatively stable due to 
convective mixing and moisture effects. This confirms that
seasonality controls vertical temperature structure.
- Freezing Level (FL) Behavior: The mean freezing level is 
within station elevation range  confirms regression is 
physically grounded and mountain network captures the 0°C 
isotherm. Daytime shows higher FL and steeper LR while 
nighttime shows lower FL and weaker LR  diurnal control
Summary
The Caucasus network exhibits a stable vertical 
temperature structure with clear seasonal asymmetry. The 
observed cool season LR provide strong evidence for 
elevation-dependent warming, particularly in winter 
conditions. These results highlight seasonal atmospheric 
processes in shaping mountain climate change.
